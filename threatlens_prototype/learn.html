MODULE 01

Phishing

Recognize fake messages, links and requests designed to trick you.

What is phishing?

Phishing is a social-engineering attack where someone pretends to be a trusted person or organization to make you reveal information, click a harmful link or take an unsafe action.

Main warning signs

  • Unexpected messages or attachments
  • Urgent or threatening language
  • Suspicious sender addresses
  • Links leading to unusual domains
  • Requests for passwords, OTPs or financial details

What should you do?

  1. Pause instead of reacting immediately.
  2. Check the sender and actual website domain.
  3. Verify through an official channel.
  4. Report or delete the suspicious message.
👨‍🏫 Teacher Learning Extension For educators

How to explain it

Teach phishing as a “pause and verify” habit. Ask learners to identify the sender, urgency, link/domain and information being requested before deciding what to do.

Classroom activity

Show a safe sample message and ask learners to circle three red flags. Then let them explain which clue made them suspicious.

Key takeaway

Trusted branding is not proof of authenticity. Encourage learners to verify through a known official website or contact method.

MODULE 02

Privacy & Personal Data

Learn what information should be protected and how to share safely.

What is privacy?

Privacy means having control over your personal information and deciding who can access or use it. Online actions can reveal more information than you intend.

Information to protect

  • Passwords and OTPs
  • Banking and payment details
  • Exact location and private contact details
  • Personal documents and account credentials
  • Private photos or sensitive conversations

Safe privacy habits

  1. Use strong, unique passwords.
  2. Enable multi-factor authentication where available.
  3. Review app permissions and account visibility.
  4. Think before posting or sharing personal information.
👨‍🏫 Teacher Learning Extension For educators

How to explain it

Help learners separate information into public, personal and sensitive categories. Emphasize that sharing less information reduces unnecessary exposure.

Classroom activity

Give learners everyday examples such as a school form, social post or app permission and ask: “What should be shared, with whom, and why?”

Key takeaway

Privacy is an ongoing habit. Review account visibility, permissions, passwords and multi-factor authentication regularly.

MODULE 03

Cyberbullying

Recognize harmful online behavior and respond safely and respectfully.

What is cyberbullying?

Cyberbullying is repeated or harmful behavior carried out through digital platforms, such as humiliating messages, targeted harassment, spreading harmful content or excluding someone online.

Warning signs

  • Repeated insulting or threatening messages
  • Sharing embarrassing content without consent
  • Targeting someone in group chats or social media
  • Impersonation or harmful rumors

How to respond

  1. Do not join or escalate the harassment.
  2. Save relevant evidence when safe to do so.
  3. Block and report harmful accounts or content.
  4. Talk to a trusted adult, teacher or appropriate support person.
👨‍🏫 Teacher Learning Extension For educators

How to explain it

Focus on recognizing harmful patterns, protecting the person targeted, and responding without escalating the situation or sharing harmful content further.

Classroom activity

Discuss a fictional group-chat scenario. Ask learners what they could do as the target, a bystander and a trusted adult or teacher.

Key takeaway

Support, reporting and safe documentation matter. Teachers should encourage learners to seek trusted help rather than handle serious harassment alone.
